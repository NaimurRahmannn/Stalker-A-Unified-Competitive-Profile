# STALKER

> A unified technical growth profile for developers who learn, compete, and
> build across many platforms.

---

## The Idea

A developer's growth is usually scattered across many places.

Codeforces may show competitive programming progress. Kaggle may show data
science work. CTFtime may show security participation. Hackathon platforms may
show project-building experience.

STALKER brings those scattered signals together into one dashboard and one
shareable profile.

## What We Are Building

STALKER is a platform where users can connect their public handles from
different technical platforms, sync their activity, and see a clearer picture of
their progress over time.

Instead of only showing raw statistics, STALKER focuses on:

- Growth over time
- Consistency across platforms
- Milestones and achievements
- Competitive and technical activity
- A single shareable developer identity

## Platform Signals

| Area | Example Platforms | What It Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitive programming | Codeforces, CodeChef, AtCoder, LeetCode | Problem solving, rating, contests, consistency |
| Data science | Kaggle | Datathon activity, notebooks, competitions |
| Cybersecurity | CTFtime | CTF participation and team activity |
| Hackathons | Devpost, DoraHacks | Projects, building experience, collaboration |

## First Working Slice

The first working version focuses on Codeforces.

A user can register, log in, connect a Codeforces handle, sync real Codeforces
data, and store normalized stats in the backend. This gives the project a real
end-to-end journey before expanding to more platforms.

## Long-Term Vision

The goal is to make STALKER a technical growth dashboard: a place where a
developer can track their journey, compare progress across platforms, and share
a public profile that represents more than a resume or a single coding-site
account.

## Guiding Question

**What does my technical journey look like across all the platforms where I
learn, compete, and build?**
